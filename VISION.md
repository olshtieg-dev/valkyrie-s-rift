## The game in one sentence

A small girl in a suit of armor, her wings broken, climbs out of Hel, up the world tree, into the sky realm, and breaks into Valhalla.

## Pillars

1. **It's a climb.** The whole game is one vertical ascent from the bottom of the cosmos to the top. Progress means *up*. Levels, camera, and world layout should all reinforce upward movement.
2. **Mostly linear, well developed.** The main path is a deliberate, carefully crafted sequence of levels, not a sprawling open world.
3. **Small detours, real rewards.** The path occasionally lets you skirt off to the sides. Those side routes are where new abilities are found, and abilities unlock new levels further along the climb.
4. **Small hero, broken wings.** She's small and armored, and she can't truly fly. What her wings can still do is limited, and that limitation is part of the struggle of the climb.

## The protagonist

- A small girl in a suit of armor.
- Her wings are broken.
- Her goal: escape Hel and climb all the way up to break into Valhalla.

## The world: a few realms, bottom to top

| Order | Realm | Role in the climb |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hel** | The start. She climbs out of it. |
| 2 | **Yggdrasil, the world tree** | The long ascent between the depths and the sky. |
| 3 | **The sky realm** | Above the tree, the approach to Valhalla. |
| 4 | **Valhalla** | The summit. She doesn't arrive; she *breaks in*. |

Only these few realms. New content deepens them rather than adding realms.

## What this rules out

- Horizontal-first level design where the climb is incidental.
- Open-world or hub-and-spoke structure that replaces the main linear path.
- Abilities handed out on the main path when they belong at the end of a side route.
- Unrestricted flight.
- Additional realms beyond the ones above.
